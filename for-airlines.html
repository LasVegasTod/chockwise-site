Chockwise does not replace your airline's ops systems or the airport's AODB or resource-management system, which assign the next arrival under the airport's rules. It is the planning layer beside them: how every gate is really used, at every airport you fly to.

Gate use is worked out from flights, so "own", "shared" and "common use" describe how gates are actually used, not lease terms.

Questions

Is Chockwise gate management software for airlines?

Yes, for the planning side of gate management: gate utilization, where a new flight fits, which gate could become yours and how gates are used at stations you don't control. It is not a day-of-operations system and does not push live gate assignments into an airport's AODB.

Can we use it for station gate planning at airports we don't operate?

Yes. Gate use is worked out from flight data, so you see your own stations, your competitors' gates at the same airport, and airports you want to enter, all measured the same way.

Does it replace an airport's AODB or resource-management system?

No. Those systems run the day at one airport and assign gates and stands under the airport's rules. Chockwise sits beside them as a view of how gates are actually used across many airports.

Do we need cameras or an IT integration to start?

No. There is no hardware to install and nothing to connect to airline or airport systems.

How does Chockwise decide which airline has a gate?

A gate counts as an airline's when that airline, with its regional partners, flies at least 70% of the arrivals and departures there over the days measured. That describes actual use, not lease terms.

Who uses it inside an airline?

Network planning, station operations, gate planning teams and ground operations leaders who prepare schedule changes or gate allocation meetings.
